# Theory of Power

## The idea, in one paragraph

"Power" is not one thing. At least four frameworks are needed before you can say anything precise about a case — and collapsing them into "the powerful vs. the people" is how analysis goes vague.

## Comparative table

| Thinker  | Power is…                                                 | Key mechanism                                                                                                | What it explains best                                                                    | What it misses                                                       |
| -------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Foucault | Dispersed, productive, not just repressive                | Discipline, governmentality, power/knowledge — institutions shape what can be said and known                 | Why people police themselves; how expertise becomes control                              | Deliberate strategy by identifiable actors                           |
| Gramsci  | Hegemony: rule by consent as well as coercion             | Cultural leadership — the ruling bloc makes its worldview feel like common sense                             | Why losers defend the system that loses for them; media and education as battlegrounds   | Economic structure can be underplayed                                |
| Bourdieu | Capital in different fields, converted between them       | Social, cultural, economic, and symbolic capital; habitus — dispositions that feel natural                   | Why the same person is powerful in one room and nobody in another                        | Change — the system can look too self-reproducing                    |
| Lukes    | Three faces: decision, agenda-setting, preference-shaping | 1st face: winning votes. 2nd: keeping issues off the table. 3rd: shaping what people want in the first place | The jump from "who won" to "what was never debated" to "why people want what hurts them" | The third face is hard to prove without assuming false consciousness |

## How to use this in a Scalpel

Map a case across all four before concluding: Who decided (Lukes 1)? What never reached the agenda (Lukes 2)? What preferences were shaped beforehand (Lukes 3)? Which institutions made that shaping feel natural (Foucault, Gramsci, Bourdieu)? A case that only works on one face is a weaker case.

## The sharpest objection

Frameworks are lenses, not findings. Quoting Foucault doesn't prove a power relation exists in this case — it tells you where to look. Every Scalpel still has to do the case work: documents, dates, incentives, who profits.
